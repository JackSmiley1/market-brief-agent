Long-Horizon

Claude forecasts each business five years out; code ranks them. Top 5 held.

Virtual portfolio: no orders placed. Not investment advice.

How it works

Every quarter, after earnings season, Claude reads each company's current fundamentals and recent news and forecasts two things: how fast revenue will grow over the next five years (with a low, middle, and high estimate) and what its profit margin will be in year five. It never says "buy" or "sell."

Code then values each company the same way: today's revenue grown at the middle estimate, times the forecast margin, at one uniform price-to-earnings multiple, compared with today's market value. That gives an implied yearly return, used only to rank. The five highest-ranked stocks are held, equally weighted, until the next quarter.

The idea comes from FutureSearch's study of the S&P 500, whose central lesson is that short-term moves are close to unpredictable while long-run business forecasts may not be. Results here will take a year or more to mean anything.

Track Record

The paper-trading experiment that came before the research tools, kept in full as a case study.

All trades were simulated on a paper-trading account. No real money. The daily strategy ended Oct 20, 2026.

What Was Tested

From Aug 18 to Oct 20, 2026, Claude made daily market calls on a fixed watchlist and placed simulated trades on them (Alpaca paper account, no real money), graded every night against real prices and against simply holding SPY.

On the day it was stopped (159 trades), its per-trade return versus SPY was below zero with 95% confidence, and a mid-test rule change hadn't fixed it. Claude also graded its own calls more generously than prices did (… vs … price-checked). The daily strategy ended on Oct 20, 2026; the complete record is below, unedited.

What came out of it: daily price moves look close to unpredictable from prices and news, which is why the project now focuses on research and on the longer-horizon experiment.

About

What Great Lakes Investments is, how it works, and what it isn't.

Educational research only. Not investment advice or a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

What it is

Great Lakes Investments is a student-built AI market research project. Type in a stock, ETF or major coin and it shows how it has performed over 1 day, 10 days, 30 days, year to date, 1 year and 5 years, how that compares with the market and its sector, and a written breakdown of why it moved.

It started as an automated paper-trading experiment. That experiment is finished and kept in full under Track Record, including the result: it didn't beat the market.

How a lookup works

1. Prices: up to five years of daily prices come from Alpaca's market data, adjusted for splits and dividends.

2. Numbers: code, not AI, computes every return, the comparisons with SPY and the sector, volatility, drawdown and the 52-week range.

3. News: the last ten days of company headlines and the latest market headlines come from Finnhub.

4. Breakdown: Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, reads those numbers and headlines and writes the explanation. It is told to use only the numbers it's given, to cite the news it relies on, and to say plainly when nothing in the news explains a move.

How it keeps itself honest

Numbers come from data, not the AI. The performance tables are calculated directly from prices. The written analysis can be wrong; the numbers it sits next to are not invented.

"No clear catalyst" is an allowed answer. Every breakdown is labeled with how strong its explanation is.

No recommendations. It explains what happened. It never says buy, sell or hold.

The failed experiment stays public. The Track Record tab shows the paper-trading loss and how Claude graded itself more generously than prices did.

Limits

Fund lookups cover ETFs; mutual fund share classes such as VFIAX aren't in this data source. Crypto covers about 20 major coins, and its history may be shorter than five years. Prices can be delayed by 15 minutes or more during the trading day. To keep costs bounded, each visitor can generate a few new breakdowns a day, and every breakdown is shared and reused for the rest of that day.

Built with

Claude (Anthropic) for analysis, Alpaca for market data, Finnhub for news and company data, a Cloudflare Worker for lookups, GitHub Actions for the scheduled long-horizon track, and GitHub Pages for this site. The code, a candid status memo and the roadmap are public on GitHub.
